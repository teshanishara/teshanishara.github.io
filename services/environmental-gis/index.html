Ecological Science Meets Geoprocessing

Defensible Ecological and Coastal Modeling for Resilience Planning

Environmental systems require specialized scientific understanding that generic mapping technicians lack. Led by a specialist with an honors background in Fisheries and Marine Sciences and academic research experience with University of Southampton initiatives, GeoPhoenix bridges ecological domain mechanics with spatial computing.

We quantify physical hazards along vulnerable coastlines through the USGS Coastal Vulnerability Index (CVI) framework—integrating regional coastal geomorphology, shoreline erosion/accretion rates, coastal slope, relative sea-level rise, mean significant wave height, and mean tidal range. For terrestrial water resources, we generate hydro-conditioned Digital Elevation Models (DEMs), delineating catchments, Strahler stream orders, and Height Above Nearest Drainage (HAND) flood-hazard surfaces.

Rigorous Methodology

Our Environmental Modeling Protocol

Adhering to defensible oceanographic and hydrological standards.

Stage 01

Geophysical Baseline Compilation

Acquiring calibrated bathymetric data, Copernicus DEMs (30m/12m), tidal station harmonic constituents, wave buoy time-series, and coastal geology maps.

Stage 02

Hydro-Conditioning & Preprocessing

Correcting DEM sink depressions, burning stream centerlines through artificial bridge barriers, and segmenting coastline reaches into uniform 100m–500m analysis transects.

Stage 03

Multi-Variable Risk Indexing

Classifying parameters into standard 1–5 risk rankings and computing compound index scores (e.g., CVI = sqrt((a*b*c*d*e*f)/6)) with sensitivity analysis.

Stage 04

Spatial Deliverables & Peer-Review Prep

Delivering georeferenced spatial vectors, classified hazard rasters, and methodology documentation suitable for regulatory compliance or scientific journal review.
